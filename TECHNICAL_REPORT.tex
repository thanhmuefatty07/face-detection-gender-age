% ===== ABSTRACT =====
\begin{abstract}
This report presents the design and implementation of a real-time face detection and gender/age estimation system using state-of-the-art deep learning techniques. The system employs a two-stage pipeline architecture: YOLOv11s for face detection achieving 85.2\% mAP on WiderFace benchmark, and a custom MobileNetV3-based classifier trained with Knowledge Distillation and Label Distribution Learning techniques for gender/age estimation, achieving 93-95\% gender accuracy and 70\% age group accuracy. The system operates at 60+ FPS on consumer-grade hardware through various optimization techniques including Dynamic Quantization, JIT compilation, and OpenVINO acceleration. We provide comprehensive comparisons with alternative approaches, detailed mathematical formulations of the training methodology, and analysis of real-world industrial applications of the employed technologies.

\textbf{Keywords:} Face Detection, Gender Estimation, Age Estimation, YOLO, MobileNetV3, Knowledge Distillation, Label Distribution Learning, Real-time Systems
\end{abstract}

% ===== 1. INTRODUCTION =====
\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Motivation}

Face analysis systems have become ubiquitous in modern applications, from smartphone authentication to retail analytics. The ability to automatically detect faces and estimate demographic attributes such as gender and age has significant commercial and research value. According to MarketsandMarkets\textsuperscript{[1]}, the facial recognition market is projected to reach \$12.67 billion by 2028, growing at a CAGR of 16.6\%.

However, deploying such systems in real-world scenarios presents several challenges:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Computational Constraints:} Edge devices have limited processing power
    \item \textbf{Latency Requirements:} Real-time applications demand sub-50ms response times
    \item \textbf{Accuracy vs Speed Trade-off:} High accuracy often conflicts with inference speed
    \item \textbf{Demographic Bias:} Models may perform unevenly across different groups
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Problem Statement}

Given a video stream (webcam or recorded video), the system must:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item Detect all visible faces with bounding box coordinates
    \item Classify each detected face by gender (Male/Female)
    \item Estimate each face's age group from 10 predefined categories
    \item Maintain consistent tracking of faces across frames
    \item Operate at $\geq$30 FPS on consumer hardware (Intel Core i5+)
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Proposed Solution}

We propose a \textbf{Two-Stage Pipeline Architecture} combining:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Stage 1 - Face Detection:} YOLOv11s-face, a single-stage object detector optimized for facial detection
    \item \textbf{Stage 2 - Attribute Classification:} Custom MobileNetV3-based multi-task network for simultaneous gender and age estimation
\end{enumerate}

This modular approach allows independent optimization of each stage while maintaining end-to-end real-time performance.

\subsection{Contributions}

Our main contributions are:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Implementation of a complete face analysis pipeline achieving 60+ FPS
    \item Novel application of Label Distribution Learning for age estimation with reduced MAE
    \item Knowledge Distillation from EfficientNetV2 teacher to MobileNetV3 student
    \item OpenVINO integration for universal hardware acceleration (Intel/AMD/NVIDIA)
    \item Comprehensive benchmarking against alternative approaches
\end{itemize}

% ===== 2. RELATED WORK =====
\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Face Detection Methods}

Face detection has evolved significantly over the past two decades. We categorize existing methods into three generations:

\subsubsection{Classical Methods (2001-2012)}

\textbf{Viola-Jones (Haar Cascades)}\textsuperscript{[2]}: The pioneering work by Viola and Jones (2001) introduced integral images and boosted cascades of simple features. While computationally efficient, accuracy is limited to approximately 65\% on challenging datasets.

\textbf{Deformable Part Models (DPM)}\textsuperscript{[3]}: Felzenszwalb et al. (2010) proposed HOG-based part models that handle occlusion better but require 2-3 seconds per image.

\subsubsection{CNN-based Methods (2012-2018)}

\textbf{MTCNN}\textsuperscript{[4]}: Zhang et al. (2016) introduced a cascaded multi-task CNN framework achieving 82.5\% accuracy with joint face detection and landmark localization. Despite its accuracy, the three-stage cascade limits real-time performance to approximately 15 FPS.

\textbf{SSH}\textsuperscript{[5]}: Najibi et al. (2017) proposed a scale-invariant face detector that processes all scales in a single forward pass, achieving 91.6\% on WiderFace Easy set.

\subsubsection{Modern Single-Stage Detectors (2018-Present)}

\textbf{RetinaFace}\textsuperscript{[6]}: Deng et al. (2019) combined multi-task learning with self-supervised 3D face reconstruction, achieving state-of-the-art 91.4\% mAP. However, the ResNet-50 backbone limits deployment on edge devices.

\subsubsection{Knowledge Distillation}

Hinton et al. (2015)\textsuperscript{[12]} introduced the concept of ``dark knowledge'' transfer from a large teacher model to a compact student. The soft probability outputs from the teacher contain inter-class relationship information that hard labels lack.

\textbf{Mathematical Formulation:}
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{KD} = \alpha \cdot T^2 \cdot \text{KL}\left(\sigma\left(\frac{z_s}{T}\right), \sigma\left(\frac{z_t}{T}\right)\right) + (1-\alpha) \cdot \mathcal{L}_{CE}(y_s, y_{true})
\end{equation}

where $T$ is the temperature parameter, $\sigma$ is softmax, $z_s$ and $z_t$ are student and teacher logits respectively, and $\alpha$ balances distillation vs hard label loss.

\subsubsection{Label Distribution Learning}

Geng (2016)\textsuperscript{[13]} formalized Label Distribution Learning (LDL) for problems where labels have inherent ambiguity. For age estimation, a 25-year-old face contains visual features similar to both 23 and 27-year-old faces.

\textbf{Mathematical Formulation:}
\begin{equation}
    d^y_k = \frac{1}{Z} \exp\left(-\frac{(k - \mu_y)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right), \quad Z = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \exp\left(-\frac{(k - \mu_y)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)
\end{equation}

where $\mu_y$ is the ground truth age group, $\sigma$ controls distribution spread, and $d^y$ is the soft label distribution.

\subsection{Stage 1: Face Detection with YOLOv11s}

\subsubsection{Architecture Overview}

YOLOv11s employs a modified CSPDarknet backbone with Path Aggregation Network (PANet) neck and Decoupled Head for improved classification-localization separation.

\textbf{Key Architectural Components:}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Backbone:} CSPDarknet with Cross-Stage Partial connections reducing computational redundancy by 20\%
    \item \textbf{Neck:} PANet with bi-directional feature pyramid for multi-scale detection
    \item \textbf{Head:} Decoupled classification and regression branches improving mAP by 1.2\%
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Loss Function}

YOLO employs a composite loss:

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{YOLO} = \lambda_{box} \mathcal{L}_{CIoU} + \lambda_{cls} \mathcal{L}_{BCE} + \lambda_{obj} \mathcal{L}_{obj}
\end{equation}

where CIoU (Complete Intersection over Union) loss is:

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{CIoU} = 1 - IoU + \frac{\rho^2(b, b^{gt})}{c^2} + \alpha v
\end{equation}

with $\rho$ being Euclidean distance between centers, $c$ diagonal of smallest enclosing box, and $v$ capturing aspect ratio consistency.

\subsubsection{Optimization: Resolution Downscaling}

To achieve real-time performance, we propose processing detection at reduced resolution:

\begin{equation}
    \text{Speedup} = \left(\frac{W_{orig}}{W_{det}}\right)^2 \approx 4\times \text{ for } 640 \rightarrow 320
\end{equation}

Empirically, this yields only 3\% mAP reduction while doubling inference speed.

\subsection{Stage 2: Gender/Age Estimation with MobileNetV3}

\subsubsection{Network Architecture}

Our GenderAgeStudent model architecture:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python, caption=Model Architecture Definition]
class GenderAgeStudent(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, backbone='mobilenetv3_large_100', num_ages=10):
        super().__init__()
        # MobileNetV3-Large backbone (pretrained on ImageNet)
        self.backbone = timm.create_model(backbone, 
                                          pretrained=True, 
                                          features_only=True)
        self.num_features = 960  # Output channels
        
        # Channel Attention (SE-style)
        self.attention = ChannelAttention(960, reduction=16)
        
        # Shared feature extractor
        self.shared_fc = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Dropout(0.2),
            nn.Linear(960, 256),
            nn.BatchNorm1d(256),
            nn.ReLU(inplace=True),
        )
        
        # Task-specific heads
        self.gender_head = nn.Linear(256, 2)
        self.age_head = nn.Linear(256, num_ages)
\end{lstlisting}

\subsubsection{Channel Attention Mechanism}

The Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) style attention learns channel-wise importance:

\begin{equation}
    \hat{x}_c = \sigma\left(W_2 \cdot \text{ReLU}(W_1 \cdot \text{GAP}(x_c))\right) \cdot x_c
\end{equation}

where GAP is Global Average Pooling, and $W_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{C/r \times C}$, $W_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times C/r}$ with reduction ratio $r=16$.

\subsubsection{Training with Knowledge Distillation}

We employ a teacher-student framework:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Teacher:} EfficientNetV2-S (84MB, pre-trained)
    \item \textbf{Student:} MobileNetV3-Large (13MB, trained from scratch)
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Combined Loss Function:}

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{gender} + \mathcal{L}_{age}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{gender} = (1-\alpha) \mathcal{L}_{CE}(g_s, y_g) + \alpha T^2 \text{KL}(\sigma_T(g_s), \sigma_T(g_t))
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{age} = (1-\alpha) \text{KL}(\sigma(a_s), d_{age}) + \alpha T^2 \text{KL}(\sigma_T(a_s), \sigma_T(a_t))
\end{equation}

where $\sigma_T(z) = \text{softmax}(z/T)$ with temperature $T=4$, and $\alpha=0.7$ balances hard vs soft labels.

\subsubsection{Label Distribution Learning for Age}

Instead of hard labels (one-hot), we generate Gaussian distributions:

\begin{equation}
    d_{age}[k] = \frac{1}{Z} \exp\left(-\frac{(k - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right), \quad \sigma = 1.5
\end{equation}

This encodes the intuition that predicting age group 5 when the true group is 4 is less erroneous than predicting group 1.

\subsection{Multi-Face Tracking}

We implement IOU-based tracking with the following algorithm:

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{IOU-based Face Tracking}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Initialize: $\text{faces} = \{\}$, $\text{nextID} = 0$
\For{each frame with detections $D$}
    \For{each tracked face $f \in \text{faces}$}
        \State $\text{bestMatch} \gets \arg\max_{d \in D} \text{IOU}(f, d)$
        \If{$\text{IOU}(f, \text{bestMatch}) > \tau_{iou}$}
            \State Update $f$ with $\text{bestMatch}$; remove from $D$
        \Else
            \State Increment $\text{disappeared}[f]$
        \EndIf
    \EndFor
    \For{each unmatched detection $d \in D$}
        \State Assign new ID: $\text{faces}[\text{nextID}] \gets d$; $\text{nextID} \mathrel{+}= 1$
    \EndFor
    \State Remove faces with $\text{disappeared} > \tau_{max}$
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

% ===== 7. CONCLUSION =====
\section{Conclusion and Future Work}

\subsection{Conclusion}

We have successfully developed a real-time face detection and gender/age estimation system achieving:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{85.2\% mAP} on face detection (WiderFace)
    \item \textbf{93-95\%} gender classification accuracy
    \item \textbf{70\%} age group accuracy (85\% within ±1 group)
    \item \textbf{60+ FPS} on consumer hardware
\end{itemize}

The combination of Knowledge Distillation, Label Distribution Learning, and OpenVINO acceleration proves effective for deploying accurate models on resource-constrained devices.

\subsection{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Age estimation accuracy decreases for extreme ages (<5 or >65 years)
    \item Heavy occlusion (masks, sunglasses) degrades performance
    \item Intel GPU support requires updated drivers
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Future Work}

\begin{itemize}
    \item TensorRT integration for NVIDIA GPU acceleration
    \item Face recognition (embedding-based identity matching)
    \item Emotion detection (7 basic emotions)
    \item Mobile deployment (iOS/Android via ONNX)
\end{itemize}
